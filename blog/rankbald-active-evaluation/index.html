01: The evaluation goal changes the acquisition

A precise ability estimate does not automatically give a reliable ranking.

Traditional adaptive evaluation asks which item will improve our estimate of a model's latent ability. RankBALD asks a different question: which item will tell us whether one model truly outranks another?

The central claim

Item selection should be aligned with the downstream decision. When the decision is a leaderboard ordering, acquisition should directly target uncertainty near pairwise ranking boundaries.

Ability estimation

Reduce uncertainty around each model's scalar capability parameter. This is useful, but it can spend budget where the final ordering is already obvious.

Ranking recovery

Resolve uncertain pairwise comparisons, especially between nearby models whose posterior ability distributions overlap.

When is a ranking uncertain?

Move the two posterior means and their shared uncertainty. The probability of A outranking B depends on their separation relative to posterior uncertainty.

Key distinction

Uncertainty matters where it can change the decision.

Reducing the variance of a clearly first-place or last-place model may improve its ability estimate without changing the leaderboard. A smaller reduction near a close pair can be far more valuable for ranking.

02: The measurement model

Item Response Theory explains where an evaluation item is informative.

In the two-parameter logistic IRT model, every language model has a latent ability, and every evaluation item has a difficulty and a discrimination parameter. An item is most informative when its difficulty lies near the ability region we need to resolve.

Probability of a correct response
\[p(U_{kj}=1\mid\theta_k)=\sigma\!\left(a_j(\theta_k-b_j)\right)\]

Model k has ability \(\theta_k\). Item j has difficulty \(b_j\) and positive discrimination \(a_j\).

Fisher information
\[\mathcal{I}(\theta_k,a_j,b_j)=a_j^2p_{kj}(\theta_k)\left(1-p_{kj}(\theta_k)\right)\]

The response is most sensitive near the middle of the logistic curve. Items that are far too easy or far too difficult add little information.

From a response matrix to item and model parameters

In a real calibration set, we begin with a pool of models answering a pool of questions. The binary response matrix is observed, while model abilities and item parameters are unknown. A 2PL estimator fits them jointly by increasing the likelihood of the observed correct and incorrect responses. The paper uses item parameters calibrated in advance from 102 language models and holds them fixed during active evaluation; this lab visualizes that upstream calibration step.

Joint 2PL calibration objective
\[\max_{\boldsymbol\theta,\mathbf a,\mathbf b}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left[U_{kj}\log p_{kj}+(1-U_{kj})\log(1-p_{kj})\right],\qquad a_j>0\]

Difficulty \(b_j\) locates the midpoint of the item response curve. Discrimination \(a_j\) controls its slope. Ability \(\theta_k\) locates each model on the same latent scale.

How discrimination is learned

A discriminative question separates nearby ability levels sharply.

During joint maximum-likelihood fitting, a question receives a larger positive discrimination estimate when its response pattern changes consistently from incorrect to correct as estimated model ability increases. A noisy or nearly uniform response pattern produces a flatter fitted curve and a smaller discrimination estimate.

Pairwise ordering variable
\[Z_{s,t}^{r}=\mathbf{1}[\theta_s^{r}>\theta_t^{r}]\]

The variable records which model in a pair has the greater latent ability at acquisition round r.

Observable joint response
\[Y_{s,t,j}^{r}=(U_{sj}^{r},U_{tj}^{r})\in\{0,1\}^{2}\]

Abilities are latent, so the evaluator learns about ordering through the two models' correctness outcomes on candidate item j.

RankBALD acquisition score
\[\operatorname{RankBALD}_{j}^{r}=\frac{\sum_{(s,t)\in\mathcal{P}}\omega(d_{s,t})\bar\pi_{js}^{r}\bar\pi_{jt}^{r}I(Z_{s,t}^{r};Y_{s,t,j}^{r}\mid p_E^{r})}{\sum_{(s,t)\in\mathcal{P}}\omega(d_{s,t})\bar\pi_{js}^{r}\bar\pi_{jt}^{r}+\varepsilon}\]

The score combines pairwise mutual information, difficulty alignment, and greater weight for nearby models. The paper uses 64 Monte Carlo posterior samples to estimate the required expectations.

What to notice

RankBALD concentrates evidence around close competitors.

A generic information criterion can favor an item that sharpens several ability estimates. A ranking-aligned criterion instead rewards an item that is informative for an unresolved pairwise boundary, even when its global ability information is smaller.

At 100 items

RankBALD records validity 24.23, variance 5.81 in the main table, and saturation 0.861. The statistical analysis reports significant improvements over Fluid Benchmarking for validity and variance at this budget.

07: What this changes

Better evaluation can come from changing what we ask each item to teach us.

RankBALD does not modify the IRT response model or the posterior inference procedure. It changes the acquisition objective so the selected items serve the actual downstream decision: recovering a reliable ordering of language models under a limited budget.

For leaderboard design

Spend fewer evaluations on comparisons whose order is already clear, and more on items that distinguish close competitors.

For evaluation research

Define acquisition utility from the decision the benchmark must support, rather than treating uncertainty reduction as universally interchangeable.

Scope

The method depends on a calibrated probabilistic measurement model.

The study uses fixed item parameters in a 2PL IRT framework and a Laplace approximation for posterior ability uncertainty. Future work can investigate richer response models and even stronger focus on closely competing systems.
